The SEC Climate Rule: Scope 3 Solid Waste Emissions

  • Home
  • Blog
  • SEC Climate Disclosures

As federal disclosure mandates tighten, commercial operators must look beyond Scope 1 (direct fuel) and Scope 2 (purchased utility) emissions. Scope 3 emissions—representing the indirect carbon footprint of your supply chain and downstream waste hauling operations—are coming under intense board-level scrutiny.

Solid waste sent to local municipal landfills is one of the most common Scope 3 emission sources. Landfill decomposition releases heavy methane (CH4) gases, which carry a global warming potential 28 times higher than carbon dioxide (CO2). Without detailed, physical baseline audit data, companies are forced to utilize generic EPA emission factor estimators, which often overestimate real carbon impacts by up to 150%.

Understanding the haulage carbon offset

To record accurate Scope 3 numbers, facilities must track three primary data points:

  1. Hauling Distance: The exact mileage log of collection trucks traveling from your facility docks to downstream incinerators or sorting yards.
  2. Material Composition: Organic wastes (food, un-treated wood, fibers) decompose into methane, whereas plastics, glass, and metals act as inert landfill materials.
  3. Broker Verification: Documenting that plastics or paper sold to recycling mills are processed into secondary products, allowing for raw material offsets.
"Without physical baseline audit weight logs, corporate sustainability teams cannot verify landfill diversion claims to satisfy strict federal ESG criteria, exposing operators to greenwashing penalties."

Securing Verified ESG Audit Logs

Physical, multi-category waste sorting audits insulate clients from regulatory audits. By separating cardboard, wrapping sheets, organic scraps, and metal off-cuts, we generate a verified composition sheet. These baseline statistics feed directly into carbon accounting tools, allowing companies to document TRUE diversion progress and claim legitimate credits.
